Why salary divided by 2,080 underprices freelance work

An employee can be paid for meetings, internal administration, holidays, and quiet periods. An independent professional funds all of that time from a smaller pool of billable hours. A sustainable rate therefore starts with the income you want to keep, then accounts for operating expenses, tax, capacity, and profit.

Rate = [(take-home goal + expenses) ÷ (1 − tax rate)] ÷ annual billable hours × (1 + profit margin)

Estimate billable capacity honestly

If you work 40 hours each week, you probably cannot invoice all 40. Proposals, marketing, bookkeeping, client email, learning, and gaps between projects consume capacity. Use your own tracked history when available. For planning, a billable share around 60–75% is more defensible than assuming every working hour generates revenue.

Break-even is a floor, not a quote

The minimum result covers the assumptions you entered. The recommended result adds resilience for slow months, equipment replacement, and business growth. Market positioning, urgency, specialized expertise, and the value of the outcome may support a higher price. If the market will not support your floor, change the offer, audience, cost structure, or income target rather than silently working at a loss.

From hourly rate to project price

Use the hourly figure internally to evaluate project economics. For a fixed project, itemize realistic hours, include meetings and handoff, add an uncertainty allowance, direct costs, and payment fees. The main ScopeRate project calculator handles those layers and produces a printable estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How many billable hours should a freelancer use?

Use tracked history if possible. Many freelancers plan around 20–30 billable hours within a 40-hour work week because administration and sales are necessary but not directly billable.

Should tax be included in an hourly rate?

Yes, as a planning reserve. Tax treatment varies by country and business structure, so verify the actual percentage with a qualified local professional.

Is the recommended rate what clients will pay?

Not automatically. It is the rate your assumptions require. Compare it with the value you create and the market you serve.
