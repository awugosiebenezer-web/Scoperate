A day rate prices reserved capacity

When a client books a day, smaller fragments of that day may be hard to resell. A day rate should therefore reflect both productive hours and the opportunity cost of reserving your schedule. It does not need to equal exactly eight times an hourly price.

Day rate = hourly floor × billable hours/day × (1 + booking premium)

Why half-days usually cost more than half

A half-day still requires scheduling, context switching, communication, and setup. Many independent professionals cap half-days at a defined number of hours and price them at more than 50% of a full day.

Discount only for real commitment

A modest weekly reduction can make sense when consecutive booked days lower sales and administration effort. Apply it only when dates and payment are committed. Avoid discounting tentative volume that may never arrive.

Define what a day includes

State the working window, maximum hours, whether meetings and travel count, overtime treatment, cancellation window, and what the client must provide. A day rate limits time; it does not promise unlimited deliverables.

Compare with project pricing

Use day rates for workshops, production days, on-site work, and flexible capacity. Use fixed project fees when the outcome is well-defined and your efficiency should not reduce compensation.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours are in a freelance day rate?

Often six to eight billable hours, but define your own limit explicitly. Breaks, travel, and availability expectations should also be stated.

Should weekly bookings be discounted?

Only if a firm block reduces your sales and administration cost. A small 0–10% adjustment is more sustainable than a deep discount.

Can I charge overtime?

Yes, if the agreement defines the daily limit and overtime price before the work occurs.
