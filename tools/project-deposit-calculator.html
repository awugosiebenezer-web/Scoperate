A deposit reserves capacity and limits credit risk

Freelancers often commit calendar time before delivery. An upfront payment confirms that the client can follow the payment process and reduces the amount of completed work exposed if an invoice becomes late.

Common project payment structures

For short projects, 50% before work and 50% before final handoff is simple. Longer work may use 30% to schedule, 30–40% at a meaningful milestone, and the balance before launch or final files. Very small standardized services may reasonably be paid in full upfront.

Final payment = total fee − deposit − milestone payments

Connect money to observable progress

A milestone should be something both sides can recognize, such as approval of strategy, completion of design, or delivery to staging. Avoid dates that depend entirely on client feedback unless the agreement explains how delays affect the schedule.

Put the payment process in writing

State due dates, accepted methods, late-payment consequences, pause rights, cancellation terms, and when final files or intellectual-property rights transfer. Requirements differ by jurisdiction, and this calculator is not legal advice.

Do not treat the whole deposit as profit

It funds future delivery. Reserve applicable tax, direct project costs, and enough working capital to finish the engagement. A healthy payment schedule aligns cash collection with the effort and risk of each phase.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 50% deposit normal for freelancers?

It is common for short fixed-fee projects and new client relationships. Larger projects often use a smaller deposit followed by milestone invoices.

Is a deposit refundable?

That depends on the written agreement and applicable law. Describe what happens if either party cancels and obtain local legal advice where needed.

When should work start?

After the agreement is signed and the deposit has cleared. State this clearly so a tentative conversation is not mistaken for a reserved start date.
