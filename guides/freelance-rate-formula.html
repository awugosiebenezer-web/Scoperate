The sustainable freelance rate formula

Updated August 19, 2026 · ScopeRate editorial

A salary divided by 2,080 hours is not a freelance rate. Employees are paid during administration, holidays, and quiet periods; freelancers must fund those hours from fewer billable hours.

Step 1: choose an after-tax income goal

Use the annual amount you need to pay yourself—not gross revenue. Add annual business expenses such as equipment, software, insurance, accounting, and training.

Step 2: gross up for tax

A planning formula is: (income goal + annual expenses) ÷ (1 − tax rate). Tax treatment varies by country and entity, so use a local professional for the actual reserve.

Step 3: estimate billable capacity honestly

Start with working weeks after holidays and sick time. Multiply weekly hours by your realistic billable percentage. Many independent workers spend substantial time on proposals, marketing, email, and administration. If you can work 40 hours but bill 25, use 25.

Step 4: divide, then add resilience

Minimum sustainable rate equals required gross revenue divided by annual billable hours. Add a profit margin for slow months, investment, and growth.

Worked example

Suppose the income goal is $70,000, expenses are $10,000, and the planning tax rate is 25%. Required gross revenue is $106,667. At 25 billable hours for 46 weeks, capacity is 1,150 hours. The floor is about $93/hour. Adding 20% profit gives a recommended internal rate near $112/hour.

Market reality still matters

Your cost creates a floor, not automatic client willingness to pay. If the market will not support it, improve positioning, sell higher-value outcomes, reduce overhead, or change the target segment—do not silently work at a loss.
