How much deposit should a freelancer request?

Updated August 19, 2026 · ScopeRate editorial

A deposit reserves capacity, funds startup work, and tests whether the client can follow a payment process. It is not a substitute for a clear contract.

Common starting points

  • 50% upfront: practical for short projects and new clients.
  • 30–40% upfront: useful for larger projects followed by milestones.
  • 100% upfront: can fit very small, standardized services.
  • Monthly or milestone billing: better for multi-month engagements.

Match payment to exposure

Ask how much unpaid work could accumulate if a payment is late. Structure milestones so that exposure stays tolerable. For a three-phase project, 30% to schedule, 40% after the central milestone, and 30% before final handoff is easier to manage than waiting for 70% at the end.

State when work and rights transfer

Write the due date, accepted payment methods, late-payment process, pause rights, cancellation terms, and whether final files or intellectual-property rights transfer only after full payment. Requirements differ by jurisdiction.

A deposit should accompany a schedule

Explain that work begins only after both signed agreement and cleared deposit. If the client delays feedback or materials, the delivery date should move and your reserved capacity may need rescheduling.

Do not spend the tax portion

A deposit is revenue, not free cash. Reserve applicable tax and retain enough to complete the promised work. Use staged invoicing to keep cash flow aligned with delivery.
