Fixed price vs hourly: which protects your margin?

Updated August 19, 2026 · ScopeRate editorial

Neither model is universally better. The right choice depends on who can control the scope, how accurately you can estimate the work, and who should carry the risk.

Use hourly pricing when the path is uncertain

Hourly billing fits maintenance, consulting, research, iteration, and projects whose requirements will evolve. The client buys access to your time rather than a promised outcome. Add a weekly cap or budget range so cost remains predictable.

Use fixed pricing when the outcome is defined

A fixed fee works when deliverables, acceptance criteria, dependencies, revision limits, and exclusions can be written clearly. It rewards expertise: finishing efficiently does not reduce the value of the result. But the freelancer accepts estimation risk, so a buffer belongs in the price.

A practical decision rule

  • Clear deliverable, proven process: fixed fee.
  • Unknown technical path: hourly or paid discovery first.
  • Large project with separable outcomes: milestone fees.
  • Ongoing access and predictable capacity: retainer with defined limits.

How to calculate a defensible fixed fee

Start with realistic hours for every deliverable, including meetings and handoff. Multiply by your sustainable hourly rate. Add direct costs and a 10–50% uncertainty allowance based on the quality of the brief. Account for payment fees and profit. The hourly rate is your internal floor—not necessarily the number you lead with.

Protect either model in writing

State what “done” means, how many revisions are included, what the client must supply, how delays affect dates, and how changes are approved. Hourly work needs reporting and a budget cap; fixed work needs a change-order mechanism.
